Market recap, Fri Oct 30

The S&P 500 fell 1.8% to 7,580, the Nasdaq fell 2.9% to 25,437, and the Dow fell 1.1% to 51,770. The Nasdaq fell the most because it is weighted toward technology, and tech was the worst sector at -4.9%. Industrials lost 1.6% and communications lost 1.3%. Energy gained 1.9%, staples gained 1.4% and utilities gained 0.7%. Money moved toward sectors whose earnings depend less on the economy or on tech spending.

The main cause was a set of weak earnings reports that raised worries about how much companies are spending on AI. Amazon missed earnings and fell about 7% during the day. Its AI spending pushed free cash flow negative, which means it spent more on investment than it brought in from operations. CEO Andy Jassy said AI spending would be meaningfully higher in 2027, so the pressure on cash is expected to continue. Google fell about 12%, its worst day as a public company, after an earnings miss and the same AI concerns. Meta fell 2% and Walmart rose 2%.

Intel was the largest single move. It fell 28.6% to 65.76 after a weak fourth-quarter forecast and a slump in PC demand, even though its quarterly earnings beat expectations. The market priced the outlook rather than the quarter that had just ended.

For your holdings, GEV fell 3.4% to 857.87 and BAC fell 2.2% to 50.01. Both fell less than the Nasdaq. Your watchlist took larger losses: AMD fell 10.9%, Dell 10.8%, Bloom Energy 8.6%, SPCX 6.4%, Palantir 6.3%, TSMC 6.0% and Nvidia 4.2%. Coverage described the hyperscaler spending worry as spreading across these names. The question to follow is whether Amazon and Google keep raising AI spending. Lower spending from them would hurt the chip and hardware suppliers on your list, and higher spending would keep pressure on the cash flow of the companies doing it.

Market recap, Thu Nov 19

US stocks fell sharply on Thursday, with the S&P 500 down 2.0% to 7,176, the Nasdaq down 2.2% to 23,879 and the Dow down 2.2% to 49,097. The decline was broad. Even the best sectors fell: energy lost 0.7%, utilities 1.2% and consumer staples 1.4%. The weakest were consumer discretionary at 2.8%, communications at 2.7% and materials at 2.1%. When the defensive sectors fall too, it usually means selling was market-wide and not limited to a few industries.

The main news was in chips. TSMC and Samsung reportedly suspended some chip deliveries to Chinese customers because of new US export rules, and a separate report said the US is closing a loophole that let China buy Nvidia chips. Export rules limit which products a company can sell to which countries, so a tightening can remove sales these companies had counted on. The headlines do not say how large the lost sales would be. Nvidia is also under an interim CEO during a leadership transition, and the interim CEO said "we've got this." Leadership changes add uncertainty because investors do not yet know who will set strategy. Nvidia fell 3.3% to $145.98.

AMD was the biggest decline among the stocks you follow, down 7.7% to $489.19. The short seller Hunterbrook published a report tying AMD's firmware to a blacklisted Chinese server maker. A short seller profits when a stock falls, so its reports deserve scrutiny, but a link to a blacklisted company can bring regulatory and legal risk. The facts do not say whether the claim has been confirmed. Intel moved the other way, rising roughly 5% to 8% in the headlines after it named its first outside customer for its 18A manufacturing process, and its CEO said "I just cannot supply enough."

For your positions, GE Vernova fell 2.1% to $756.28 and Bank of America fell 0.7% to $48.57, both less than the market. Your watchlist did worse than the index: Amazon fell 3.9%, SPCX 3.5%, Alphabet 3.5%, Morgan Stanley 3.4%, Meta 3.3% and Goldman Sachs 2.9%. The facts given do not tie those declines to specific news, so they look like part of the broad selloff. Of everything you hold or watch, only AMD has a company-specific reason in the headlines for falling more than the market.

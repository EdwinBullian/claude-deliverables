Market recap, Mon Oct 26

Stocks rose on Monday, led by technology. The S&P 500 gained 0.7% to 7,677, the Nasdaq gained 0.8% to 26,169, and the Dow gained 0.3% to 52,362. Technology was the best sector at +1.7%, followed by industrials at +1.4% and consumer at +1.0%. Communication services fell 1.6%, and staples and utilities were roughly flat. The Dow trailed the other two indexes, which is consistent with tech doing most of the work.

Several of the stocks you watch moved with that tech strength. Intel rose 4.0% after New Street upgraded it, citing a multi-year shortage of server processors. A shortage means demand for the chips exceeds supply, which usually supports pricing and sales for the companies that make them. AMD gained 2.8% and Nvidia gained 2.4% in the same session. Tesla rose 5.2% after Barclays upgraded it and called a possible SpaceX merger a key part of the bull case. An upgrade means an analyst now expects the stock to do better, and it often pulls in buyers on the day.

Among your holdings, GE Vernova rose 4.9% to 855.12, and Bank of America slipped 0.1% to 50.52. The facts here give no company-specific reason for either move. Bloom Energy, Dell, Morgan Stanley and Goldman Sachs also rose between 2.0% and 5.5%.

Meta fell between roughly 0.6% and 1.8% depending on the report, after announcing plans to raise up to $95 billion by selling stock to fund its AI spending. That includes a $35 billion offering now and up to $60 billion more planned for 2027. Selling new shares dilutes existing owners, meaning each share represents a smaller slice of the company. Meta also stopped buying back its own stock in 2026. The market is reacting to how much money AI infrastructure is requiring, and that bears on other big spenders in the sector. Separately, Microsoft rose 1.1% after offering voluntary buyouts to about 60,000 employees, a cost-cutting move.

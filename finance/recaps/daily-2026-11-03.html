Market recap, Tue Nov 3

The S&P 500 rose to 7,569, up 0.4%, and the Nasdaq gained 0.8% to 25,372. The Dow fell 0.4% to 51,787. When the Nasdaq rises and the Dow falls, the gains are concentrated in a narrow group of stocks rather than spread across the market. Tech led the sectors at +1.3%, followed by communications at +1.2% and industrials at +0.9%. Staples fell 2.1%, materials fell 2.0%, and energy fell 0.8%. The facts here don't give a reason for the drop in staples or materials, so the cause is unknown. Staples are slow-growth companies, so a sector that large falling that much means money moved away from them.

Your holding GE Vernova rose 6.5% to $918.20. The company agreed to pay $9 billion for Trench Group, which makes equipment for the electric grid. The deal is a large bet that demand for electricity and grid equipment will keep growing, and the market rewarded it. Bank of America, your other holding, gained 0.2% to $49.66, which is roughly flat.

Among the stocks you watch, Bloom Energy rose 9.1% to $214.01. Barclays upgraded it, and it announced a partnership with California Resources aimed at 1 gigawatt of fuel-cell capacity. An upgrade is an analyst raising their view of the stock, and a large capacity deal gives investors a concrete source of future revenue. Dell rose 4.0%, AMD and SPCX each rose 3.6%, Microsoft rose 2.7%, Meta rose 2.4% and Nvidia rose 2.3%. Those moves fit tech leading the day. Ford fell 2.3% to $12.31, and nothing in the facts explains why.

Two large tech names reported earnings and fell even though they beat estimates. Apple dropped after warning that memory chip costs will squeeze its profit margin, which is the share of revenue left as profit. Palantir fell after its contract growth slowed, even with revenue up 96%. Both show that the market is pricing future growth and costs, not just the latest quarter. For your watchlist, that means a good earnings report can still lead to a falling stock if the outlook disappoints.

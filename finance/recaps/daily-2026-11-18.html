Market recap, Wed Nov 18

The S&P 500 closed at 7,324, down 0.2%, with the market split. The Nasdaq rose 0.4% to 24,430, while the Dow fell 1.1% to 50,198. Consumer stocks led the sectors at +1.8%, ahead of tech and utilities at +0.3% each. Energy fell 3.3%, health care 2.2%, and financials 1.1%. When one index rises and another falls, a few sectors are driving the result and the market is not moving as one.

The financials drop touched both the stock Eddie holds and several he watches. Bank of America, which he holds, fell 1.1% to $48.91. JPMorgan fell 1.8%, and Morgan Stanley fell 1.3%. GE Vernova, his other holding, fell 1.0% to $772.22.

Nvidia was the main story and fell 2.9% to $150.96. It has now fallen five days in a row and has given back the gain it posted after earnings. Those earnings showed Q3 revenue of $115.8 billion, and the company raised its outlook to growth of about 75%. The selling follows Jensen Huang leaving as CEO, with Colette Kress named interim CEO. Summit Insights cut its rating on the stock because of uncertainty after Huang's exit. A House committee has stepped up its investigation and asked Kress to testify, and Huang is set to face senators. The business numbers are strong, but investors are pricing in leadership and regulatory risk, and that risk is likely to keep Nvidia's stock under pressure until the testimony and the CEO question are settled.

Other watched names moved sharply. AMD rose 5.3% to $530.14 and Amazon rose 3.8% to $267.74, and no reason was given for either. Dell fell 3.0% and Bloom Energy fell 1.4%. SPCX rose 1.3% to $143.50 after a report that Anthropic will pay SpaceX up to $120.5 billion for computing capacity through 2029. A contract of that size gives the company a large, defined stream of future revenue.

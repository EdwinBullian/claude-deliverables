Market recap, Fri Nov 27

US stocks rose broadly on Friday. The S&P 500 gained 1.6% to 7,297, the Nasdaq rose 2.3% to 24,130, and the Dow added 1.2% to 50,775. The Nasdaq gained more than the other two because it holds more technology companies. Technology was the best sector at +2.8%, followed by consumer at +2.1% and communications at +2.0%. Health care, utilities and staples were the weakest, but each still rose, by 0.3% to 0.4%. The facts don't say why the whole market rose.

The main company news was about Nvidia. Headlines reported that Nvidia is halting chip sales to four Southeast Asian countries. Bloomberg also reportedly found Nvidia's Blackwell chips in a Chinese data center, and there were reports of new US restrictions on China's access to AI chips. This matters because export controls limit where the most advanced chips can be sold, and each new report of chips reaching China makes tighter rules more likely. One headline said Nvidia fell on the day, but no price for it is available here.

Dell is on your watchlist and rose 6.7% to $316.96. A headline said Dell faces a possible AI server shipment halt because of the Nvidia restrictions, and Dell's servers depend on Nvidia chips. The stock rose anyway, so the market did not treat that risk as serious today. A separate headline said Dell posted record results but that Wall Street sees a catch, which points to profit margins on AI servers. Palantir rose 5.2% to $172.17 after co-founder Peter Thiel disclosed a $122 million share purchase. A large insider purchase can suggest confidence in the company, but it doesn't predict the stock's direction.

The rest of your watchlist also rose. Those gains were Bloom Energy +6.6%, Intel +5.4%, Tesla +5.2% at $400.00, SPCX +4.8%, AMD +4.6% and Amazon +4.0%. Among your holdings, GE Vernova gained 2.8% to $735.31 and Bank of America gained 1.1% to $49.07. Tech-heavy names rose the most, which fits the sector results. The Nvidia export story is the main risk to follow for the chip and server names.

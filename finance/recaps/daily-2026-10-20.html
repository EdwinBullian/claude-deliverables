Market recap, Tue Oct 20

The S&P 500 rose 0.3% to 7,713 and the Dow gained 1.0% to 52,496. The Nasdaq was flat at 26,425, down less than 0.05%. The three indexes differ because the Dow and S&P were lifted by sectors outside technology, while tech fell 0.3%. Health care led at +2.4%, followed by real estate at +1.7% and utilities at +1.3%. Industrials and communications each gained only 0.1%. No single cause for the rotation was reported, so it is best read as money moving toward steadier sectors while tech lagged.

Among the stocks Eddie holds, Bank of America rose 0.5% to $50.64. GE Vernova fell 2.0% to $890.33, and no news explained the drop. Among the banks he watches, Morgan Stanley rose 1.1% and Goldman Sachs rose 1.0%, so financials had a decent day. Meta fell 1.6% and Alphabet fell 1.1%, which fits the weak tech sector. Bloom Energy also fell 1.6%. Tesla rose 1.5%, Dell rose 1.3% and TSMC rose 1.2%.

SpaceX is the main event to watch. It reports its first earnings as a public company today, and the result isn't known yet. The stock is down 3.0% going in. First reports matter because they set how investors judge the company's growth and costs from here. Tesla also reported, and coverage focused on Elon Musk's comments about a possible SpaceX merger. That is speculation, not an announced deal.

Two analyst moves are worth noting. Apple fell 1.1% after a downgrade citing iPhone shipment concerns and slowing sales growth. Downgrades matter because they can change how many investors want to own the stock. JPMorgan upgraded Intel, citing strong management execution, and there are reports of a foundry win, meaning a customer to make chips for. The details of that win are unclear, so it is worth reading before drawing conclusions.

Market recap, Thu Nov 12

US stocks closed slightly lower. The S&P 500 fell 0.3% to 7,592, the Nasdaq fell 0.4% to 25,366, and the Dow fell 0.4% to 52,297. Energy was the only sector with a clear gain, up 1.5%. Materials fell 1.3%, communications 1.0% and industrials 0.9%. Tech slipped 0.3% and utilities were flat. A decline this small means the market did not move much as a whole, and the sector gaps were larger than the index move.

The main story was Nvidia, which fell 4.3% to $166.19. Jensen Huang is out as CEO, and CFO Colette Kress is interim CEO. Nvidia has said the search for a permanent CEO will take three to four months and called it "pretty urgent." Until a successor is named, investors have less clarity on who sets the company's direction. A separate headline asks what the change does to US-China AI talks. Those talks concern export controls, which limit what chips American companies can sell to China, so a leadership change at the largest chip designer could matter to the outcome. The other chip names on your list also fell: TSM lost 1.9% and INTC lost 2.1%. AMD went the other way, up 5.9% to $585.30. No reason for that move appeared in today's news.

Alphabet rose 1.8% to $298.70 after Google committed $10 billion to Anthropic at its IPO. That is a large investment on Alphabet's balance sheet and shows how much it is putting into AI. Separately, SpaceX announced $74 billion of AI data-center expansion in Texas and Arizona. SpaceX is not on your lists, but the spending shows how much money is going into AI infrastructure.

Of your two holdings, GEV rose 1.3% to $818.49 and BAC fell 0.3% to $50.51, so they roughly matched the market. Among your other watch names, TSLA gained 1.7%, while BE lost 3.6% and F lost 2.9%. No news explained those three moves.

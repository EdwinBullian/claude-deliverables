Market recap, Mon Nov 16

The S&P 500 fell 1.9% to 7,417, the Nasdaq fell 2.5% to 24,510, and the Dow fell 1.4% to 51,608. The Nasdaq fell more than the other two because it holds more technology companies. Tech was the worst sector at -3.2%, followed by energy at -2.0% and industrials at -1.9%. Only staples (+0.3%) and utilities (+0.2%) rose. Those are the sectors people tend to own for steady demand, so the split shows selling concentrated in growth and technology names.

The main reported cause was a new US export-control action that tightens rules on AI chips. Export controls limit which chips US companies can sell abroad, so tighter rules can reduce future sales for chip makers. Headlines said AMD and Broadcom shares sank on the rules, and CoreWeave and Nebius also dropped. Another headline said the administration is advancing new limits on semiconductor exports, and that President Trump acknowledged "different views" among his aides. Separately, the Financial Times reported that Jensen Huang is considering a return to Nvidia, with Kress serving as interim CEO ahead of Nvidia's earnings. A change in who runs the largest AI chip company adds uncertainty before that report.

The watchlist fell hard. Bloom Energy fell 7.7%, Intel and Dell each fell 7.0%, TSMC fell 6.3%, AMD fell 5.6%, Palantir fell 4.5%, and Nvidia and Alphabet each fell 4.1%. Dell's drop was described as part of an AI hardware selloff, which suggests the move reached companies that build AI systems as well as chip designers. Palantir raised its 2026 guidance and reported 96% revenue growth, yet it still fell, so on this day the sector move outweighed good company news. AMD also completed its World Labs acquisition and named Fei-Fei Li chief scientist, but the price still fell.

Of the two holdings, GE Vernova fell 4.9% and Bank of America fell 1.8%. The reporting gives no specific reason for either move. GE Vernova's drop was larger than the S&P's and Bank of America's was about the same. Export rules and Nvidia's earnings are the two items most likely to shape how the AI-related watchlist names trade next.

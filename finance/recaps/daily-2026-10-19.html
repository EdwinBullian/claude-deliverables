Market recap, Mon Oct 19

The S&P 500 rose 0.4% to 7,691 and the Nasdaq gained 1.1% to 26,431, while the Dow slipped 0.1% to 51,966. The gap between the Nasdaq and the Dow means the gains were concentrated in growth and technology names rather than spread across the market. Consumer stocks led the sectors at +1.8%, followed by tech at +1.3%. Utilities fell 2.0%, real estate fell 1.2%, and financials fell 0.8%.

Amazon was the biggest move among the stocks Eddie watches, up 5.0% to 262.45. Morgan Stanley named it a top pick and said it expects Amazon Web Services, the cloud business, to grow 47% in 2027. The shares had their best day in three months. An analyst call like this matters because it changes what investors expect for the company's fastest-growing segment, and stock prices move on expectations about future earnings.

Intel rose 2.4% to 92.03 after JPMorgan upgraded it, saying its confidence is building. The stock had fallen 14% over the past week, so part of the move may be a recovery from that drop. The same day, Nvidia won CPU business from Modal that had previously gone to AMD and Intel. That is a competitive loss for Intel, but the upgrade outweighed it in the price. TSMC gained 1.8% to 420.22. Its third-quarter profit rose 69%, but that report is already out, so it is likely already reflected in the price. Palantir (+4.4%) and Tesla (+3.8%) also rose, and the facts here give no specific reason for either.

Financials were the weak spot for the holdings and the watchlist. Bank of America fell 1.6% to 50.39 and JPMorgan fell 1.8% to 333.53, in line with the sector's 0.8% drop. Eddie's other holding, GE Vernova, fell 1.4% to 908.44. That happened even though it landed five grid equipment reservations from Venture Global, so good company news did not stop the stock from falling today. Ford also fell 2.0% to 12.18. No cause is given for any of these declines, so they should not be tied to a single event.

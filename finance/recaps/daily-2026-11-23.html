Market recap, Mon Nov 23

Stocks rose sharply today. The S&P 500 gained 4.7% to 7,237, the Nasdaq gained 6.5% to 24,090, and the Dow gained 3.0% to 49,595. Technology led by a wide margin, with the tech sector up 9.0%, followed by consumer at 4.0% and industrials at 3.9%. Energy fell 1.1% and staples slipped 0.1%, so the rally was concentrated in growth and technology companies. Real estate rose 1.0%, the smallest gain among the sectors that rose. A move of this size in a broad index in one day is rare, and the Nasdaq beating the Dow shows that most of the gain came from technology.

Nvidia drove much of the move. The stock rose 31.5% to $165.05 after its board reinstated Jensen Huang as CEO, 12 days after he left the role. Reports say Trump gave the board assurances that Huang was not a national security threat. Nvidia is one of the largest companies in the index, so a gain this large lifts the S&P 500 and Nasdaq by itself. One report says an SEC accounting probe may be a bigger overhang than the CEO return. That is a legal and regulatory risk on a stock that just rose about 31% in a day. Nvidia is on your watchlist, not a holding.

Other watchlist names rose with it. Dell gained 20.5%, Bloom Energy 18.1%, Intel 16.3%, TSMC 14.1%, Tesla 11.5%, Palantir 11.1% and AMD 6.8%. Intel's gain followed news that it landed its first customer for its 18A-P chip manufacturing process, which matters because Intel's foundry business needs outside customers to justify its investment. Tesla's gain followed the rollout of FSD V15, its driver-assistance software. Amazon agreed to buy Decart's AI technology and team in a reported $7.5 billion deal, though no price move for Amazon was given.

Your two holdings rose more modestly. GE Vernova gained 8.2% to $756.71, in line with the strong industrial and technology tone of the day. Bank of America gained 2.3% to $48.14, a smaller move that fits a financial stock in a rally led by tech. The gap between your holdings and the watchlist names shows how unevenly the day's gains were spread. The Nvidia and Intel stories are tied to company-specific news, so they do not by themselves say the whole market has changed direction.

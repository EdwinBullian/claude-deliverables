Market recap, Fri Nov 13

The S&P 500 fell 0.4% to 7,562 and the Nasdaq fell 0.9% to 25,136. The Dow rose 0.1% to 52,348. The gap between the Nasdaq and the Dow came from sector weights. The Nasdaq holds more technology companies, and tech was the worst sector at -2.0%. Consumer and industrials each slipped 0.4%. Staples gained 1.4%, communications 1.1% and energy 0.9%. Staples are companies that sell everyday necessities, so money moving there while tech falls is a pattern of investors favoring steadier businesses.

The biggest moves among your watchlist names were in chips and hardware. AMD dropped 8.8% to $534.06, Dell fell 7.2% to $344.53 and Bloom Energy fell 4.9% to $191.99. No specific cause has been reported for those three, so treat the size of the drops as a fact and the reason as unknown. Nvidia fell 2.0% to $162.85 after CEO Jensen Huang resigned. One report says the stock has lost 22% over the past 10 days. A CEO change matters because investors price in how well the next leader will run the company. Intel fell 2.5% to $59.66 after a $1.6 billion guidance miss, which means the company's own forecast came in below what it had told investors to expect. It also lost the executive described as Nvidia's dealmaker.

Tesla fell 1.8% to $390.22. A report says Tesla and SpaceX could announce a merger as soon as December, and the incoming Texas attorney general has said a deal would get rigorous scrutiny. A merger of that size would change what Tesla shares represent, and a regulatory fight could delay or block it. Separately, Apple is reported to be running a surprise CEO search with Tim Cook at the center. No Apple price was provided, so the market reaction is not known.

Meta was the one standout watchlist gain at 2.9% to $641.69, with no reason reported. Of your two holdings, Bank of America rose 1.0% to $51.02, in line with the market's move away from tech. GE Vernova fell 1.9% to $803.13, which is close to the tech drop even though it is not a tech company.

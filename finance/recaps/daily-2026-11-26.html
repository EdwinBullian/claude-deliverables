Market recap, Thu Nov 26

The Dow rose 0.3% to 50,183, while the Nasdaq fell 0.6% to 23,586. The S&P 500 finished at 7,183, slightly lower. The split between the indexes comes from what each one holds. The Nasdaq is weighted toward technology, and tech was the worst sector at -0.8%, so it fell the most. Materials led at +2.1%, with real estate and communication services each up 0.9%. Health care fell 0.6% and consumer stocks fell 0.2%. The facts don't say why materials rose, so I won't guess at a cause.

Of the two stocks you hold, neither moved much. GE Vernova fell 0.8% to $715.00. Bank of America closed at $48.54, essentially unchanged. A small move in a holding on a day when the index barely changed says little about the company. It mostly shows these two did not follow the tech selloff.

Most of the larger moves were in your watchlist. Nvidia fell 3.9% to $149.30 and Amazon fell 3.1% to $244.43. Bloom Energy fell 2.2%, Alphabet fell 2.1%, Taiwan Semiconductor fell 2.0% and AMD fell 1.8%. Meta rose 3.4% to $609.89 and Ford rose 1.8% to $12.17. The facts don't give a reason for any of these moves, and the stock-specific headlines I have don't line up with them. One headline reports that Taiwan Semiconductor was upgraded to a strong buy, yet the stock fell. Another reports an analyst upgrading Alphabet to a hold after a 35% decline, and that stock fell too. An analyst rating is one firm's opinion, and a stock can drop on the same day for other reasons. The chip names Nvidia, AMD and Taiwan Semiconductor all fell together, which fits tech being the weakest sector, but that is a pattern, not a stated cause.

Two other headlines are worth knowing, though neither concerns a stock you follow. Intel dropped sharply after disappointing earnings, and SpaceX and Tesla were reported to be assembling deal teams ahead of a merger. Chip weakness and merger talk are the kind of news that can pull attention away from the names you watch, so check whether either affects your Nvidia and AMD positions.

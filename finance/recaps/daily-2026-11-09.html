Market recap, Mon Nov 9

US stocks rose broadly on Monday. The S&P 500 gained 2.4% to close at 7,550, the Nasdaq rose 2.7% to 25,253, and the Dow rose 2.1% to 52,390. The Nasdaq gained the most because it holds more technology companies, and tech was one of the strongest sectors. Industrials led at +3.8%, tech followed at +3.7%, and consumer stocks rose 2.9%.

Every sector rose, but the gains were uneven. Energy was weakest at +0.5%, then staples at +0.9% and financials at +1.4%. When the weakest groups still rise, the market is broadly higher and not being carried by a few names. Even so, the money clearly went toward industrials and tech more than toward energy and staples.

Of the two stocks Eddie holds, GE Vernova rose 7.1% to $829.34, well ahead of the market, which fits industrials leading the day. Bank of America rose 2.3% to $50.55, slightly below the S&P 500's 2.4%. That matches financials trailing the index at +1.4%.

The watchlist did better than the index. AMD rose 13.3% to $508.58. A headline attributes AMD's rise to a Google Cloud deal and a Wall Street upgrade. A cloud deal can mean more chip sales, and an upgrade means an analyst now rates the stock more favorably. Bloom Energy rose 11.9%. SpaceX-linked SPCX gained 5.8%. Tesla, Palantir and Intel each gained 5.5%, Dell 5.4% and Meta 5.1%.

Nvidia moved the other way. Its board opened an investigation into CEO Jensen Huang over his ties to an alleged Chinese spy, and the stock fell. Reports put the drop between about 4% and 7%, depending on the time of the story. A board probe of a CEO matters because it raises the chance that the company's leadership changes. A separate report says Nvidia intervened before a chip probe moved to the Justice Department, which adds a legal issue on top of the CEO controversy.

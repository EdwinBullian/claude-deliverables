Market recap, Wed Oct 21

US stocks rose on Wednesday, led by technology. The S&P 500 gained 0.6% to 7,757, the Nasdaq rose 1.1% to 26,703, and the Dow added 0.7% to 52,856. The Nasdaq, which is weighted toward tech, did best. By sector, technology rose 1.7%, industrials 0.9% and financials 0.6%. Communication services fell 1.1%, real estate 0.8% and consumer staples 0.7%.

The biggest single story was SpaceX (SPCX), which reported its first earnings as a public company and rose 7.8% to $159.31. Revenue rose 161%, and headlines say analysts' estimates were off by about $1.6 billion, so the result was far better than expected. Stocks usually move most on the gap between results and expectations, which is why a miss of that size matters. On the earnings call, Elon Musk said a SpaceX and Tesla merger is under consideration and that it would be a good idea. Separate reports say the two companies formed a merger exploration committee that excludes Musk. A merger has been discussed, not agreed.

Tesla (TSLA) rose 3.4% to $386.47 on the merger talk. It reports earnings tonight, so the merger question may come up again. How a deal would work, and what each company's shareholders would receive, has not been disclosed.

The other tech names Eddie watches also rose: Bloom Energy (BE) 7.1%, Dell 5.3%, Palantir 4.7%, AMD 3.4% and Nvidia 2.5%. None of the information available gives a company-specific reason for those moves, and they fit the sector's strength. Of the two holdings, GE Vernova (GEV) rose 4.0% to $925.92 and Bank of America (BAC) rose 0.3% to $50.78. Morgan Stanley, on the watchlist, gained 3.7%, which is more than the financial sector's 0.6%. The financial sector's gain was smaller than the gains in tech and industrials, so BAC's small rise is in line with it.

Market recap, Fri Nov 6

The S&P 500 rose 0.3% to 7,377, and the gain came almost entirely from technology. The Nasdaq rose 1.1% to 24,579, while the Dow fell 0.5% to 51,286. Tech was the best sector at +1.4%, followed by communications at +0.4%. Consumer staples fell 1.3%, materials fell 0.7% and real estate fell 0.7%. When the tech-heavy Nasdaq gains much more than the Dow, it means a small group of large companies is doing most of the lifting rather than the whole market rising together.

The biggest single driver was AMD, up 11.0% to $449.04 after reporting third-quarter earnings that beat expectations. Its data center revenue rose 97%, which means sales of chips and systems to the companies that run large computing facilities nearly doubled. That matters because those customers are building out AI capacity, and AMD's results show the spending is reaching it. Other chip and hardware names on your watchlist rose in the same session: Intel gained 7.7%, TSMC gained 6.3% and Dell gained 3.7%. The facts given do not state a specific reason for those moves, though they fit with the strength in AMD and tech overall.

Tesla rose 4.2% to $358.22. Elon Musk is ending Cybertruck production to free up factory capacity for the Cybercab, Tesla's planned robotaxi. This is a change in what the company chooses to build, and the headlines note the shares only edged up after an earlier rout around the election. Palantir rose 3.5%.

The power names were weaker or mixed. Bloom Energy fell about 16% in the headline, though your watchlist table shows it up 3.4% at $173.24, so those two figures conflict and I can't reconcile them. The headline attributes the drop to midterm-related fears for AI power stocks, even though Bloom signed a 900 MW datacenter deal with NTT DATA. A headline also says GE Vernova fell on a pause in AI data center permitting, which could slow orders for its grid and power equipment, but your table shows it up 0.5% at $774.11. Bank of America, which you hold, fell 0.7% to $49.42, and a headline says other large banks fell more in a market selloff.

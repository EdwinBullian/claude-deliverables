Market recap, Wed Nov 25

US stocks ended mixed. The S&P 500 closed at 7,183, slightly lower. The Nasdaq fell 0.6% to 23,586, and the Dow rose 0.3% to 50,183. Materials was the best sector at 2.1%, ahead of real estate and communication services at 0.9% each. Technology fell 0.8% and health care fell 0.6%. Because tech makes up a large share of the S&P 500 and the Nasdaq, its decline explains why the Nasdaq fell more than the other two indexes.

The weakness was concentrated in AI-related names. Nvidia, a watchlist stock, fell 3.9% to 149.30. Headlines tie the drop to AI worries and questions about CEO Jensen Huang, and they say a rally that followed his return has unwound. Nvidia matters here because many other AI stocks trade on how investors feel about its outlook. TSMC fell 2.0% to 342.34. A headline says Taiwan was added to a US tariff list, and tariffs can raise costs and hurt demand for a chipmaker that sells across borders. AMD fell 1.8%, Amazon 3.1% and Alphabet 2.1%. The facts given don't explain those three moves.

Dell is not on the watchlist, but its story shows how the market is treating AI spending. Dell beat third-quarter earnings estimates, raised its sales forecast and reported a record $132 billion backlog. Its stock still fell on worries about the AI buildout. A beat that gets sold usually means investors expected even more, or doubt the demand will last.

Among watchlist stocks, Meta rose 3.4% to 609.89 and Ford rose 1.8% to 12.17, with no cause given for either. Of the two holdings, GE Vernova slipped 0.8% to 715.00, and Bank of America was essentially flat at 48.54. The holdings stayed steady on a day when the AI-linked watchlist names fell. Headlines also say Tesla and SpaceX are in merger talks and that Tesla fell on merger concerns. Tesla is not on the list, but it is a large company, so the news could affect sentiment around it.

Market recap, Thu Nov 5

US stocks fell sharply on Thursday. The S&P 500 dropped 4.4% to 7,354, the Nasdaq fell 5.5% to 24,315, and the Dow lost 3.4% to 51,523. The Nasdaq fell the most because it holds more technology companies, and technology was the worst sector at -8.0%. Industrials (-4.1%) and consumer stocks (-3.8%) also fell more than the market. Communications (-0.6%), utilities (-0.7%) and staples (-0.8%) held up best, so the losses were concentrated in growth and chip-related areas.

One report on the chip sell-off ties it to the midterm election results. It says the results are a "seismic shift" and calls the decline deepening. A separate report ties the midterm outcome to a tougher regulatory path for a SpaceX and Tesla merger. Tesla fell 10.8% in the table. The reports do not say why the market as a whole fell, so the midterm link is a stated explanation for these two moves and not a confirmed cause of the full decline. Other news may also have mattered. Nvidia faces a bottleneck of 600,000 chips in China, which limits how many chips it can sell there. Intel's server demand is meeting a softer outlook, which is a warning about future results.

Among the stocks you watch, the declines were steep. AMD fell 17.3% to 404.40, Bloom Energy 16.4%, Dell 14.9%, TSMC 10.8%, Intel 10.3%, Palantir 9.7% and Nvidia 9.3%. Bloom Energy fell even though NTT DATA announced a 900 MW onsite power agreement with it for data center projects. A good business announcement did not offset a market-wide sell-off. Most of these names are tied to AI and chip spending, and they dropped together, which suggests the market was reacting to the sector as a whole and not to any one company.

Of your two holdings, GE Vernova fell 7.5% to 770.51, more than the market. Bank of America fell 3.6% to 49.76, slightly less than the S&P 500. Because the sell-off was so concentrated in technology, the key thing to check next is whether the chip and AI names keep falling together or start to separate.

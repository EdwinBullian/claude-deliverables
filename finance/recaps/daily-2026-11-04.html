Market recap, Wed Nov 4

US stocks rose on the day, with the S&P 500 up 1.6% to 7,689 and the Nasdaq up 1.4% to 25,737. The Dow did best, gaining 3.0% to 53,356. The Dow holds only 30 large companies, so a bigger move there than in the S&P 500 shows the gains were concentrated in big, established firms. Midday coverage reported that Democrats won decisively in the midterms and that stocks rose, and that AMD's third-quarter earnings and revenue beat estimates. AMD's results also eased fears about AI spending, which had been weighing on the market.

By sector, consumer stocks gained 4.3%, financials 3.5% and communications 3.5%. Energy fell 4.7% and industrials fell 1.1%, while tech finished flat at -0.0%. The sector moves matter because they show where money went. Of the three stocks Eddie watches in financials, Bank of America (a holding) rose 4.0%, Morgan Stanley rose 4.2% and JPMorgan rose 4.2%, so they moved with their sector. Microsoft rose 6.7% to 555.80, Amazon rose 6.1% to 266.03 and Apple rose 5.3% to 349.51. The facts here don't say why those three rose while the tech sector as a whole was flat.

The weakest names were the power and data-center stocks. GE Vernova, a holding, fell 9.3% to 832.83. Coverage of the drop tied it to the Democratic win in Congress, which a headline said brought "incrementally more negative sentiment." The desk's reading is that the result revives worries about policy toward data centers and power. That matters because GE Vernova's growth depends on electricity demand from data centers, so a policy change could slow that demand. The same coverage said Vistra fell 12%. Bloom Energy fell 6.4% to 200.40 even though it raised its full-year outlook after revenue grew 166%. A company can raise guidance and still fall when the market is worried about the policy or sentiment around its industry.

Dell fell 8.8% to 372.36 and SPCX fell 3.8% to 135.92. No cause is given for either, so those moves are not explained.

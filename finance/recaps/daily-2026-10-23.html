Market recap, Fri Oct 23

The S&P 500 fell 0.5% to 7,624, the Nasdaq slipped 0.2% to 25,955, and the Dow dropped 1.2% to 52,215. Energy was the only sector with a large gain, up 2.6%, and tech rose 0.5%. Consumer staples (-1.9%), communications (-1.8%) and consumer discretionary (-1.7%) fell the most. The Dow fell more than the other two indexes, so the weakness was broader among large established companies than in tech. I don't have a stated reason for the sector moves.

The sharpest move among your names was Bloom Energy (BE), a watch-list stock, which fell 11.2% to $193.30. It reported a record third quarter, beat estimates and raised its outlook for the third time. It also burned $181 million in cash during the quarter. Cash burn means spending more cash than the business brings in. Investors treat that as important because the gap has to be covered from existing cash or new financing, however good the reported results look. Today's drop suggests the market weighted that cash figure more heavily than the earnings beat.

SpaceX (SPCX), also on your watch list, rose 1.9% to $147.49. Two reports are behind the move. One says SpaceX is looking at buying about 80,000 cell tower sites, and Crown Castle rose 7.5% on the talks, though no deal has been signed. The other says analysts issued a rare double upgrade. SpaceX also disclosed more than $9.9 billion of free cash flow burn in its first quarter as a public company, against an IPO that raised $85.7 billion. The rise shows that, today, investors paid more attention to the upgrades and the tower talks than to the burn.

Your two holdings both finished lower: GE Vernova (GEV) fell 1.5% to $815.00 and Bank of America (BAC) fell 1.0% to $50.56. Other financials you watch also dropped, with Morgan Stanley down 2.4% and Goldman Sachs down 1.8%, so BAC's decline matches a move across large banks. The semiconductor names AMD (+1.7%), Intel (+1.7%) and TSMC (+1.5%) rose with tech. Ford (F) fell 2.9%, and I don't have a reason for that move.

Market recap, Fri Nov 20

The S&P 500 fell about 3.7% to 6,910, the Nasdaq fell 5.3% to 22,618, and the Dow fell 1.9% to 48,147. The Nasdaq fell furthest because it holds more technology companies, and technology was the worst sector at -7.3%. Industrials fell 3.1% and consumer stocks fell 3.0%. Consumer staples was the only sector that rose, up 0.7%. Health care and energy lost less than 1%. Staples sell everyday necessities, so they usually hold up when investors are selling growth stocks.

The semiconductor group fell hardest, and Eddie's watchlist shows it. Nvidia fell 14.0%, AMD 13.2%, Dell 11.0%, TSMC 9.5%, and Intel 8.0%. AMD has the clearest cause. A short-seller report from Hunterbrook, which flagged the company's China exposure, drove it lower. A short seller profits when a stock falls, so these reports can move prices even before anyone has checked the claims. TSMC reported that it is halting some China chip orders because a new U.S. export rule took effect. That matters because China is a source of revenue for chip companies, and restrictions on it can lower their sales.

Some of the news is less clear. A headline said Jensen Huang is leaving Nvidia, but it has not been confirmed, so the cause of Nvidia's drop is not established. Intel fell even though it reportedly won its first outside customer for its 18A chip process and Microsoft AI chip business. That shows a company-specific positive was not enough to offset a sector-wide sell-off. SpaceX (SPCX) fell 7.7% and dropped below its IPO price after reports that lenders were reluctant to finance Musk's orbital AI chip plans. Bloom Energy (BE) fell 15.3% and Tesla fell 7.6%, with no specific news given for either.

For the two stocks Eddie holds, GEV fell 7.5% to $699.33 and BAC fell 3.1% to $47.04. No company-specific news was given for either, so their declines look tied to the broad market rather than anything about those businesses. Because the move was so concentrated in technology, how much each stock fell depended mostly on its exposure to chips and AI. Both holdings lost less than most of the watchlist.

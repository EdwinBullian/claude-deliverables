Market recap, Tue Nov 24

The S&P 500 fell 0.5% to 7,201, and the Nasdaq dropped 1.5% to 23,734. The Dow rose 0.8% to 50,019. The split came from sectors. Tech lost 3.3%, the worst of any sector, and industrials slipped 0.5%. Health care gained 3.0%, consumer staples 1.7%, and real estate 1.3%. Money moved out of technology and into health care, staples and real estate, which are sectors people often hold when they want steadier earnings. Tech is a large share of the Nasdaq, so that index fell the most.

Nvidia was the center of the selling. It fell 5.9% to $155.38. Headlines tied the drop to a reversal of the rally that followed Jensen Huang's return as CEO. One headline said the stock had jumped 31% on that news, and Goldman was quoted as seeing lingering uncertainty. One headline also pointed to a US blacklisting of an Nvidia customer, Megaspeed, and criticism of Nvidia's governance from Kyle Bass. The sources gave different intraday figures for the decline, so the closing figure of 5.9% is the one to rely on. Chip and hardware names on your watchlist fell with it: Dell lost 11.8%, AMD 8.9%, Intel 8.2%, TSMC 4.0%, and Bloom Energy 7.0%. The facts here don't give a reason for Dell's or Bloom's drop, only that they moved with the group.

Of your two holdings, GE Vernova lost 4.8% to $720.65, while Bank of America rose 0.9% to $48.56. The facts don't say why GE Vernova fell. Bank of America's gain fits the pattern of money leaving tech, though no source ties the two together. Ford, on your watchlist, gained 2.6% to $11.95.

SPCX fell 5.7% to $125.81. Tesla and SpaceX named five directors for a potential $3.5 trillion merger, and reports said the deal could give Musk majority control. That is a real step in a merger process, but the facts don't say whether it caused the decline. For you, the day shows how closely the AI-related names move together, and how one stock's news can pull the others down with it.

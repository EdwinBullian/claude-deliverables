Market recap, Thu Oct 22

The S&P 500 fell 1.2% to 7,663 and the Nasdaq fell 2.6% to 26,009. The Dow finished flat at 52,868. The split came from sector moves: tech fell 3.3%, communications fell 1.4% and industrials fell 0.9%, while consumer staples rose 3.3%, utilities rose 2.1% and health care rose 1.3%. Staples, utilities and health care sell things people buy in any economy, so money often moves into them when investors get nervous about growth stocks. The Dow holds fewer tech names than the Nasdaq, which is why it held steady.

The chip selloff drove much of the tech decline. A supply-constraint warning from Lam Research set it off, and the move deepened a broader selloff in AI-related stocks. Lam makes equipment used to manufacture chips, so a supply warning from it raises concern about the whole chip supply chain. Among stocks Eddie watches, AMD fell 8.1%, Intel fell 8.0% and TSM fell 4.5%. Dell fell 6.8%, Bloom Energy (BE) fell 8.7% and Amazon fell 4.6%.

Meta fell 7.0% even though its third-quarter results beat expectations. Reports said investors focused on the company's higher outlook for AI spending, meaning larger planned investment in data centers and chips, which lowers near-term profit. Headlines also pointed to a $25 billion penalty from a New Mexico judge and to a stock split. A beat can still send a stock lower when spending plans grow faster than investors expected. In other earnings news, Tesla fell about 3% to 4% after profit shrank and Elon Musk trimmed the company's sales target.

SpaceX (SPCX) fell 9.1% despite a record $14.6 billion quarter that beat Wall Street's targets in its first earnings as a public company, plus talk of a possible merger with Tesla. The news does not explain the drop, so it likely reflects the weak market for AI-linked stocks. GE Vernova (GEV), which Eddie holds, fell 10.6%, and the news gives no reason for it. Bank of America (BAC), his other holding, rose 0.6%. Most of the day's damage landed in the AI and chip names, so the next few sessions will show whether that group keeps falling or recovers.

Market recap, Tue Nov 17

The S&P 500 fell 1.0% to 7,341. The Dow fell 1.6% to 50,759, and the Nasdaq fell 0.7% to 24,328. The decline was broad, with every sector shown in the best and worst lists closing lower. Energy was the best sector at -0.5%, followed by industrials at -0.6% and consumer at -0.9%. Financials were the worst at -1.9%, followed by utilities at -1.6% and real estate at -1.5%. None of the news provided gives one reason for the drop, so it is better to treat it as a general decline than to attach a cause to it.

Financials matter to Eddie because he holds Bank of America and watches JPMorgan and Goldman Sachs. BAC closed at $49.44, down 1.3%, which was a smaller loss than the sector's. JPM fell 2.4% to $329.96 and GS fell 1.6% to $877.52. The Dow fell the most of the three indexes, and its lead over the others is consistent with the weakness in financials, though the news does not say financials caused it.

His other holding, GEV, rose 2.1% to $779.91 on a day when almost nothing else did. Among the watched stocks, TSM had the largest drop at 3.7%, closing at $359.69. SPCX rose 3.2% to $141.70. PLTR fell 2.4%, MSFT fell 2.3%, BE fell 2.1%, and F fell 2.0%.

The news adds some context. A headline says Microsoft rose on strong fiscal year-end results and a robust outlook, but the stock still closed down 2.3% today, so good results did not protect it on a broad down day. Meta fell 2.4% after Italy fined Facebook $887 million, an example of a regulatory penalty moving a stock. Nvidia is reporting earnings, and headlines also mention Jensen Huang's ouster, his wish to return, and tighter US restrictions on AI chip sales to China. One analyst firm says a guidance cut is already priced in, meaning the stock may have already dropped to reflect bad news. Nvidia's results and its leadership situation are the next things to watch for chip-related names like TSM.

Market recap, Wed Oct 28

The S&P 500 rose 0.3% to 7,744, and the Nasdaq also gained 0.3% to 26,550. The Dow rose 0.2% to 52,475. Technology led the sectors at +0.8%, followed by financials at +0.5% and energy at +0.4%. Utilities fell 0.8%, real estate fell 0.7% and communication services fell 0.4%. Because tech is the largest weight in the S&P 500, its gain was enough to lift the index even with three sectors lower.

GE Vernova, the one holding that fell, dropped 1.9% to $878.03 on a day when the company reported good news. It beat third-quarter revenue expectations, raised its 2026 guidance, said profit nearly tripled and reported a backlog of $196.7 billion. A backlog is the value of orders signed but not yet delivered, so a large one points to future revenue. The company also agreed to buy Trench Group for $9 billion, a grid equipment deal. The facts given don't say why the stock fell despite this. Bank of America, the other holding, rose 0.3% to $50.85, in line with the gain in financials.

Among the watched stocks, Bloom Energy rose 4.4% to $228.08 after its third-quarter call included a guidance increase and a capacity plan. AMD gained 3.2% to $578.44, Intel rose 1.9% to $95.53, and Ford rose 2.0% to $11.95. Intel announced a $2.3 billion purchase of a quantum computing business, which moved IonQ up 7% and Rigetti up 8%. That is a change in direction for Intel, though the facts given don't say how Intel's own shares are tied to it. Morgan Stanley rose 1.7% to $190.13 and Goldman Sachs rose 1.5% to $925.17, moving with the financials sector.

SPCX fell 2.2% to $145.23. Dell fell 1.6% to $493.79, and no cause is given for that decline. One headline described Dell as soaring on a +7.5% move, which conflicts with its -1.6% close, so treat that headline as unreliable until the cause is confirmed. Apple is also worth noting because John Ternus is about to hold his first earnings call as CEO after Tim Cook, and a change in leadership makes that report more closely watched.

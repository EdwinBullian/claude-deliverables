Market recap, Wed Nov 11

The S&P 500 rose 1.3% to 7,619, the Nasdaq gained 1.6% to 25,466, and the Dow rose 0.5% to 52,486. Tech was the best sector at +3.2%, followed by energy at +1.9% and industrials at +1.3%. Consumer stocks fell 0.1%, and real estate and health care were flat. The Dow's smaller gain fits a day when the rise came from tech and not from the market as a whole. Nasdaq and S&P 500 have more tech weight than the Dow.

Among the stocks you watch, the chip and AI hardware names moved most. Bloom Energy rose 13.0% to 209.47 and Dell rose 11.8% to 373.68. Dell's rise followed a 12% drop earlier in the week, and a market-movers story linked it to a rebound in AI-related stocks. Intel gained 7.5%, AMD 7.3%, TSMC 4.2%, and Nvidia and Tesla each 2.9%. SPCX was the one watched stock that fell, down 2.3% to 139.75. Of your two holdings, GE Vernova rose 1.4% to 808.24 and Bank of America was unchanged at 50.67. Bank of America was flat while tech jumped, so the rally did not reach financials.

Nvidia rose even though the news around it was negative. A report said it sold chips to a blacklisted Chinese university, and a Republican senator called for CEO Jensen Huang to step down. Morgan Stanley and Bank of America also removed Nvidia as a top pick. This matters because export-control or legal action could follow the report, and a leadership dispute can affect the stock. The price did not react to any of it today. Apple fell 4% after CEO Ternus called memory pricing "unprecedented," even though the quarter was a record $114.5 billion. That is a warning that rising component costs can hurt a company with strong sales, and it bears on hardware makers like Dell.

Tesla formed a special committee to review a possible all-stock merger, and the stock rose 2.9%. A committee review is an early step, so no deal is confirmed. Tesla also won a major European approval, but the headline says there is a catch and the details were not provided. Separately, Meta's infrastructure chief Santosh Janardhan will step down in early 2027. He leads the build-out of the data centers that AI spending goes toward, so his replacement is worth following.

Market recap, Thu Oct 15

US stocks fell on Friday, led by technology. The S&P 500 closed at 7,659, down 1.1%. The Nasdaq fell 2.1% to 26,140, and the Dow lost 0.3% to 52,002. Tech was the worst sector at -3.0%, followed by communications at -1.9% and consumer discretionary at -0.8%. Energy rose 2.5%, utilities 1.3% and consumer staples 1.1%.

The headlines point to a hot inflation report as the cause. Wholesale inflation in September rose by the most in four years, and one report said the Nasdaq's drop came from worry that interest rates could go up. This matters because higher rates reduce what investors will pay today for profits expected years from now. Technology companies are valued mostly on those later profits, so they tend to fall hardest when rate expectations rise. That fits the pattern of tech down sharply while energy, utilities and staples held up.

Your two holdings fell less than the market. Bank of America lost 0.9% to 51.23, and GE Vernova lost 0.6% to 921.20. Your watchlist took the larger hit. Palantir fell 7.2% to 186.03, Bloom Energy 6.3%, AMD 6.0% and Intel 5.8%. Dell dropped 4.6%, and a Barclays downgrade after the stock's large rally was in the news. Meta fell 3.7%, Alphabet 2.5% and TSMC 2.3%.

TSMC is the odd case. It reported third-quarter profit up 69% on AI chip demand, its sixth straight earnings beat, and the stock still fell. One headline put the drop at 5%, while the close was 2.3%. A strong report does not lift a stock when investors already expected it and the whole sector is selling.

For your watchlist, the day shows that these names trade as a group on rate expectations. Bloom Energy's headline also mentioned a reported $4 billion AEP fuel-cell deal to power Google, but the stock fell anyway.

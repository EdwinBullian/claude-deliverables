Market recap, Tue Nov 10

The S&P 500 fell 0.4% to 7,521, the Nasdaq fell 0.7% to 25,066, and the Dow fell 0.3% to 52,232. Technology was the weakest sector at -1.1%, while financials (+0.5%), energy (+0.4%) and communications (+0.4%) were the strongest. The Nasdaq fell more than the other two indexes because it is weighted more heavily toward technology companies.

Nvidia was the main story and closed at $168.89, down 5.5%. Headlines said the board is probing CEO Jensen Huang, and reported that an alleged Chinese spy tied to him helped with $7.5 billion of chip shipments to China. One headline put the odds that Huang leaves at 18%, and coverage has started asking who could replace him. Analysts also cut price targets, and one headline said Cathie Wood bought more than 90,000 shares after the drop. This matters because a CEO change or an export-control problem could affect Nvidia's business for a long time, which makes it a different kind of risk from an ordinary bad day.

GE Vernova, which Eddie holds, fell 3.9% to $797.04. A headline said GE Vernova, Vertiv and Bloom Energy each lost 10% to 24% over the past week in a sell-off of AI power stocks. Bloom Energy fell 4.4% today, so that group is still under pressure. These companies sell into the demand for electricity from AI data centers, so they tend to move when investors change their view of AI spending.

Other watched names were mixed. Dell fell 3.4%, Microsoft 2.2%, Intel 2.1% and Ford 1.9%, with no reason given for the last of these. Taiwan Semiconductor rose 2.0% and Amazon rose 1.5%. Bank of America, the other holding, rose 0.3% along with the financials sector. Today's weakness was concentrated in technology and AI-linked stocks, and Eddie's one bank holding was not affected.

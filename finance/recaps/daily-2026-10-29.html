Market recap, Thu Oct 29

US stocks fell modestly on the day, and the drop was concentrated in technology and communication companies. The S&P 500 slipped 0.3% to 7,720, the Dow lost 0.2% to 52,343, and the Nasdaq fell 1.4% to 26,185. The Nasdaq fell the most because it holds more technology and communication stocks. Communication services was the weakest sector at -1.9%, and technology fell 1.2%. Health care led at +2.4%, followed by real estate at +2.2% and consumer staples at +1.5%. The facts don't give a reason for that rotation.

The main cause was Alphabet (GOOGL), which fell 12.6% after its third-quarter report. Search results came in below forecasts, and spending on AI pushed its cash flow negative, with about $9 billion of cash burned. The company lost roughly $400 billion in market value, the largest one-day drop in its history. This matters because Alphabet is a very large company in both the communication sector and the Nasdaq, so its fall moved those indexes by itself. It also shows investors reacting badly when heavy AI spending isn't matched by cash coming in.

Other names you watch also fell. Amazon dropped 4.2%, Dell 3.7%, Intel 3.6%, Bloom Energy 2.5% and AMD 2.1%. Nvidia fell about 1.1% on a report that CEO Jensen Huang is caught up in a Chinese spy probe. That is a legal and regulatory risk tied to a single executive. No company-specific reason was given for the other declines, so the weakness may reflect the broader pressure on tech and AI stocks. Ford rose 3.4% and Meta rose 2.0%, and no cause was given for either.

Both holdings finished higher. GE Vernova rose 1.1% to $887.85 after agreeing to buy Trench Group for $9 billion. Bank of America rose 0.6% to $51.14. Both held up on a day when the tech-heavy parts of the market fell. For your watchlist, Alphabet's result is the number to track, since its AI spending and cash flow affect how the market views the other large AI-exposed companies.
